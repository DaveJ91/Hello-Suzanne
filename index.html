A note on Irish e-invoicing.
Week of 23 November 2026.

26 November 2026

What was new this week

There was no new Revenue eBrief, Tax and Duty Manual update, or press release on e-invoicing between Monday 23 and Thursday 26 November. The timetable published in October is unchanged. Two things did happen, and they are worth knowing.

Wednesday 25 November

The Finance Bill completed its Dáil stages

Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 finished Report Stage and Final Stage in Dáil Éireann on 25 November, and the question was carried 86 votes to 67. The Bill now goes to the Seanad. It has not yet been enacted.

This is the Bill, first published on 19 October, that contains the legislative foundation for structured domestic e-invoicing and real-time digital reporting. In the Official Report of the 25 November sitting, the resumed Report Stage debate did not separately discuss the e-invoicing clauses, and no confirmed Report Stage amendment was found that changes the published timetable or scope.

Sources: the Oireachtas Bill page, the 25 November vote, and the Official Report.

Tuesday 24 November

Revenue held its Dublin tax practitioner session

Revenue ran the scheduled in-person tax practitioner information session at An Lár, 14/15 Upper O’Connell Street, Dublin, at 11.00. The event lasted an hour and a half. It is part of the communication programme Revenue announced with its 21 and 22 October VAT Modernisation update, not a new policy announcement.

Two webinars for businesses are listed for Tuesday 1 December, at 10.30 and 14.30. A further tax practitioner session is listed for Limerick on 8 December, at Sarsfield House.

Source: Revenue’s eInvoicing webinars and information sessions page, first published on 12 November.

Monday 23 November

A conference recap, not a rule change

Chartered Accountants Ireland published a note on its Annual Tax Conference, which had taken place on Friday 20 November. The piece records remarks on preparing for e-invoicing from November 2028, and a closing session by Revenue Commissioner Ruth Kennedy on Revenue’s move toward Tax Administration 3.0. It is useful colour. It is not new guidance or legislation.

Source: Tax and Technology, 23 November 2026.

What did not change, and the last official package

Revenue issued several eBriefs this week, including on 24, 25 and 26 November. None of them concern e-invoicing or VAT Modernisation. The latest official package remains mid-October.

  • On 21 October, Revenue published VAT Modernisation: Implementation of eInvoicing in Ireland, 2026 Update.
  • On 22 October, Revenue’s VAT Modernisation press release confirmed that the phased rollout announced in October 2025 is unchanged, and pointed to draft regulations and the eInvoicing Hub.
  • Phase One still begins on 1 November 2028, for about 2,400 VAT-registered large corporates on domestic B2B invoices and real-time reporting, with all businesses required to be able to receive eInvoices from that date. Phase Two is 1 November 2029. Phase Three, the EU ViDA cross-border mandate, is 1 July 2030. Peppol remains the default network, and Revenue still intends to become Ireland’s Peppol Authority from 1 January 2027. The provisions remain subject to the legislative process.

No new Commission e-invoicing or ViDA legislative announcement dated this week was found. The Commission’s revised Explanatory Notes on the VAT e-commerce package, covering changes from 1 January 2027, are dated 24 July 2026, with an addendum dated 6 October 2026. See the Commission’s OSS guides page.
